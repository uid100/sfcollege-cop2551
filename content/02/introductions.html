Introductions

This is a place for us to get to know each other a little bit. I want to learn about you, and I want you to learn about each other. This will help us build a better learning community and make it easier for you to connect with each other when you need help or want to work together on projects.

Purpose:

  • Since we don't have the opportunity to go around the classroom and have people introduce themselves, we're going to do an online introduction and share a little bit about ourselves.

This is usually in the first module, but the first week was already getting pretty full, so I thought I'd try something a little bit different and delay it one week.
I hope this works out better for people.

Directions:

  2. (10 points)

Ideas might include: what you currently do, your major, why you are taking this class, what you would like to do after you finish your degree, hobbies, special interests, where you're originally from, where you live (general area), family, etc. You can upload pictures if you want to.

  3. (20 points)
    In the field of software engineering, from time-to-time, you may be invited to technical interviews. You may be asked for simple definitions for common concepts, perhaps the 4 pillars of object-oriented programming, unit test, or the phases of the software development life cycle. You can probably think of other common concepts that you've learned in classes. For these, your instructor, or textbook, or the Google(!) offered a working definition and it was enough to satisfy the question at the time.
    It's a good idea to take some of these basic concepts, maybe find a few example definitions, and then define them in your own words, and refine and practice them, so that they're ready for interview responses.
    For example, you know what a variable is.
    Our textbook says Variables represent storage locations in the computer's memory.
    My definition is:
    A variable is a named storage location in the computer's memory where a value of a known type and size may be stored and accessed later. The value of a variable can be changed (may "vary") throughout the execution of a program.
      In a paragraph or two, discuss:
    • Find and compare this with other definitions for a variable. Is it more or less clear, concise, or correct than what you can find in the textbook or a web search? How is it different?
    • What other other terms or concepts that you have learned that you might want to revisit and commit to memory before an interview? (encapsulation, polymorphism, abstraction, test-driven development, frameworks, ...)
